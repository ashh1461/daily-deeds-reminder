# Wird 1.8.1: the complete Tafsir al-Mizan

Everything in the app is now the full text, not a summary.

## Tafsir al-Mizan, complete
Until 1.8.0 the app held a few short thematic summaries "inspired by" al-Mizan. They are gone. The app now contains the **complete Arabic text of al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an** (Allamah Tabataba'i, all 20 volumes): the preface, every surah introduction, the commentary on every ayah, the narration discussions («بحث روائي») and the other studies, the footnotes, the page numbers of the printed edition, and the volume indices.

- **Linked to every ayah.** The book is split into 594 commentary sections, each tied to the exact ayah range it explains; all 6,236 ayat are covered. In the Quran reader, the button on **every ayah** («تفسير الميزان») opens the section that comments on it.
- **A book of its own** (العبادات → تفسير الميزان): the surahs with their sections, the preface, the volume indices, the previous/next section buttons, a font-size control and ornate-bracket styling of the quoted ayat.
- **Full-text search** across the whole book, ignoring diacritics and alef spelling, with a snippet around each match. The global search screen offers «ابحث عن … في تفسير الميزان كاملاً».
- **Nothing was dropped.** `scripts/build_mizan.py` builds the data from the complete OpenITI text and fails unless every letter of that text is kept (10,166,038 letters, 100%). The ayah ranges come from a second digitization (altafsir.com) that has them as headings; the script locates each section in the complete text, so the page markers, footnotes, indices and sub-headings are all preserved. The checks run again in the unit tests: 615 entries (1 preface, 594 sections, 20 index blocks), 74,894 paragraphs, 8,468 page markers, no leftover markup, balanced quotations, a section for every ayah, sections in Quran order, and the file's SHA-256.
- **Light on memory.** The 24 MB text is copied once to the app's private storage and each section is read from disk when opened (a few hundred KB at most); the book is never held in memory.
- The global in-memory search no longer has a "summaries" category.

## Licence correction
The notices and the About screen said the OpenITI texts were MIT-licensed. That was wrong: **the OpenITI corpus is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. This covers Mafatih al-Jinan, the Sahifa and al-Mizan.
- Attribution and the statement of changes are in Settings → About, `docs/licenses/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and the new `docs/licenses/DATA_LICENSE.md`.
- The app must stay free, with no advertising or paid features (NonCommercial); the adapted texts are shared under the same licence (ShareAlike), and the scripts that produce them are in this repository.
- The licence covers the digital corpus, not necessarily the copyright of the book. **Allamah Tabataba'i died in 1981**, so al-Mizan may still be protected in some countries, although it is distributed free of charge by Shia libraries. Get the rights holder's permission or legal advice before publishing the app in a store; this is the one piece of content that could trigger a takedown. See `docs/PLAY_LISTING.md`.

## What is still not full
- The Salat al-Ayat guide, the khums notes and the other guidance screens are short summaries of the commonly circulated rulings, not a complete fiqh book, and they say so. A full treatise would have to come from a marja's licensed text.
- Mafatih al-Jinan and the Sahifa are complete but carry no tashkeel (diacritics): the digitization has none.
- The Quran has no translation or other tafsir yet (planned).

## Known limits
- The first time the book is opened it takes a moment to copy and index the text; later opens are immediate. A full-text search over 24 MB takes a few seconds on a phone.
- The full-text search was exercised by the unit tests (the whole book, on a computer) but not on the emulator, because Arabic text cannot be typed through adb. Long sections were scrolled only briefly on the emulator.
- Not tested on a physical phone, and the emulator on this machine is slow and unstable, so timings there mean little.
- In the source, a few headings are split across two lines («قوله تعالى» and «:» appear as separate lines).
- The tables of contents of the printed volumes are shown as plain text rows.
